# Customer discovery

## Recruiting
- Target people who had the problem recently (last 3 months). Recency beats enthusiasm.
- Avoid friends and family as primary evidence; if used, tag it.
- Aim for 5–10 per segment before drawing conclusions. Stop when you stop hearing new things.

## Script rules (Mom Test)
- Talk about their life, not your idea. Ask about specifics in the past, never hypotheticals about the future.
- Good: "Tell me about the last time you…", "What did you do about it?", "What did that cost you?", "What else have you tried?", "Who else should I talk to?"
- Bad: "Would you use…?", "Do you think it's a good idea?", "How much would you pay for…?"
- Don't pitch until the end, if at all. Compliments are noise; deflect them back to specifics.
- Ask for a commitment at the end (time, intro, preorder, pilot). The answer is the strongest data point of the interview.

## JTBD switch interview (Moesta)
Reconstruct the timeline of their last switch: first thought → passive looking → active looking → deciding → first use. Map the four forces: push of the situation, pull of the new solution, anxiety of the new, habit of the present.

## Synthesis
- One row per interview: segment, recency, current solution, money/time spent, pain intensity (observed, 1–5), commitment asked and given.
- Count patterns ("6/9 use a spreadsheet; 2/9 pay for a tool"). Quote briefly; don't cherry-pick.
- Separate **did** from **said**. Only **did** and commitments move assumptions to validated.
- End with: assumptions affected, new assumptions found, next questions.
